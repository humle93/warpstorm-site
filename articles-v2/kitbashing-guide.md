## What Is Kitbashing — and Where to Find Spare Parts

Kitbashing (building "conversions") is taking parts from multiple kits — and scratch-made pieces — to create a unique model. Sources for parts:

- **Your spares box:** every kit you've ever built left useful leftovers — weapons, heads, shields, antennae
- **Swap events and club nights:** bitz trading is a hobby tradition
- **Second-hand lots:** buying a cheap bulk lot of half-built models is a parts goldmine
- **3D-printed bits:** single parts printed at home fill any gap
- **Everyday objects:** beads, buttons, springs, watch parts — the original kitbasher's secret

## Essential Tools

- **Hobby knife** with fresh blade — clean cuts, no crushed plastic
- **Plastic cement** (welds plastic together; stronger than glue)
- **Super glue** for resin and metal parts
- **Green stuff** (two-part epoxy putty) for gap-filling and sculpting
- **Pin drill + wire** for strong joins on heavy parts
- **Side cutters and needle files**

## Cutting, Pinning and Gap-Filling

1. **Cut** parts off sprues with flat surfaces — clean joins are half the job
2. **Test-fit before cement.** Plastic cement is permanent within seconds
3. **Pin heavy parts:** drill a hole in both halves, insert a wire segment, then cement — gravity is a strong enemy
4. **Fill gaps:** press green stuff into seams, smooth it with a wet sculpting tool, let it cure overnight
5. **Re-scribe panel lines** across filled areas so the repair disappears

## Creating Silhouette Balance on Converted Models

A kitbash fails or succeeds at the silhouette stage. The rule:

- **One dominant element** — the oversized feature that defines the model (a huge weapon, a banner, a mech arm)
- **Two supporting elements** — secondary additions that echo the theme
- **Everything else subordinate** — small details that reward close viewing

If three elements fight for dominance, remove one. Squint at the model: if the silhouette is clear and interesting, the conversion works.

## FAQ

**Is kitbashing expensive?**
It's the cheapest way to unique models — most bitz come from kits you already own. The tool investment (~$30–50) is the only real cost.

**Will kitbashed models be tournament-legal?**
Usually yes if dimensions stay honest and the model clearly represents what its rules say. Check event policies for proxies.

**Can I mix plastic, resin and metal parts?**
Yes — but use the right glue for each join and pin resin-to-plastic joins; super glue alone fails on heavy parts.

**How do I learn what parts fit together?**
Build one conversion from one donor kit with only weapon swaps first. Scale awareness grows with every project.
