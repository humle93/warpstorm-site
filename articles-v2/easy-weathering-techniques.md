# Easy Weathering Techniques for Miniatures

**Short answer:** Three techniques cover 90% of beginner weathering: sponge chipping for worn edges, streaking grime for vertical runs, and pigment powders for dust and mud. All three need no special skills — just dark paints, a torn foam pad and patience. Start with one technique per model, not three at once.

## Why Weathering Wins Games of a Different Kind

A clean model looks like a product photo. A weathered model looks like it *lives* in its world. Weathering is the cheapest way to make a tabletop army look experienced — and it hides beginner mistakes: uneven highlights and sloppy edges disappear under grime.

## Essential Tools

- Dark brown, dark grey and black paints
- A piece of torn packaging foam (the "sponge")
- Wash or streaking grime product
- Pigment powders (brown, ochre, grey) — optional but transformative
- Cotton buds for corrections

## Step 1: Sponge Chipping (Edges and Worn Zones)

1. Load a small piece of torn foam with dark paint — less than you think
2. Dab off most of it on paper tissue
3. Press lightly along edges that would take abuse: armour corners, hatch rims, weapon grips
4. Repeat with a lighter rust tone at 50% of the spots

**Rule:** chips follow logic. Feet, front edges, handles and hatches wear; backs and tops stay cleaner.

## Step 2: Streaking Grime (Vertical Runs)

Rain and gravity pull dirt downward. Take a very thin dark wash, touch it to the upper part of a vertical surface, and drag a single thin line downward. Let it dry fully. Two or three subtle streaks per vehicle panel are enough — more looks dirty in the wrong way.

## Step 3: Dust and Mud Pigments

1. Pick a pigment powder (earth brown for tracks and legs, grey for dry environments)
2. Apply with a soft brush to lower surfaces: boots, wheels, track guards, hems of robes
3. Fix it with a pigment fixer — or skip the fixer on models you don't handle often

Concentrate pigments where the model touches the world: the lowest 20% of the miniature.

## FAQ

**Is weathering bad for beginners?**
The opposite — it's beginner-forgiving. Weathering hides mistakes and has no "perfect line" to fail at. It's the most encouraging technique to learn early.

**How much weathering is too much?**
When you can no longer tell what the model's base colour is. Aim for 20% weathered surface, 80% original finish — even on "very grimdark" armies.

**Can I weather with just a wash?**
Yes: a targeted dark wash on edges and recesses plus pigments on the feet is a legitimate 5-minute weathering pass.

**Do pigments rub off during games?**
Unfixed pigments do. Use pigment fixer, or seal the model with matte varnish after weathering.
