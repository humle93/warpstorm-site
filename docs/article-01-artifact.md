# Best Warhammer 40K Army for Beginners

**Short answer:** The best Warhammer 40K army for beginners is **Space Marines** — tough models, forgiving rules, and the widest range of starter sets and tutorials. **Necrons** are the strong runner-up: few models per unit, simple special rules, and easy-to-paint metallic schemes. Both are cheap to start, fun to build, and forgiving while you learn the game.

## Why Space Marines Are the Best Beginner Army

1. **Forgiving on the tabletop.** Marines have good stats across the board — decent armor, decent shooting, decent melee. Mistakes in positioning don't lose you the game in turn one.
2. **Easy to paint well.** Primaris armor takes basecoat + wash + edge highlight and already looks tabletop-ready. One spray can covers most of the army.
3. **The most tutorials anywhere.** Almost every painting channel, hobby blog and competitive site has Space Marine content. You'll never get stuck.
4. **Cheapest to start.** The starter sets include a ready-made Marine force, so your first purchase is also your first army.
5. **Easy to expand.** Nearly every box sells well second-hand, so scaling up (or pivoting later) loses you the least money.

## Why Necrons Are the Best Second Choice

1. **Fewer models, bigger units.** Necron warriors are large, chunky miniatures — you assemble and paint far fewer models for a legal army than a horde faction.
2. **Simple, strong rules.** Reanimation protocols boil down to "come back on a roll." Very few keywords to memorize on game one.
3. **The easiest paint scheme in the game.** A single silver/metallic spray, green contrast paint on the gauss energy, done. Even a first-time painter gets a striking army.
4. **Instant visual identity.** Glowing green eyes and blades read as "finished" from across the table — great for morale on hobby night one.

## How to Choose: 3 Questions

- **Do you want the "poster boys"?** → Space Marines. Largest range, easiest support, safest buy.
- **Do you want to paint less and still look good?** → Necrons. One spray, one contrast, army done.
- **Do you love horde armies or tanks?** → Accept a harder start and look at Imperial Guard later — not as army #1.

## First 3 Steps After Choosing

1. Buy a starter box (Marines or Necrons — both sides are usable/tradeable).
2. Assemble 5 models only. Don't build the whole box day one.
3. Paint those 5 with a beginner method: spray primer → basecoat → wash → drybrush. Then read our [beginner painting guide](/guides/beginner-painting-guide/).

## FAQ

### Are Space Marines really the best army for beginners?
Yes. They combine easy building, forgiving rules, cheap starter sets and the largest tutorial ecosystem of any faction. You can't pick a safer first army.

### Are Necrons good for a first-time painter?
They're arguably the easiest: metallic spray base, green contrast accents, and a bold result with minimal skill. Rules are also beginner-simple.

### How much does a beginner army cost?
Expect roughly $100–150 for a starter box plus basic tools and paints. That's enough for your first full games.

### Should I buy a starter box or single units first?
Starter box. It's the cheapest entry, includes dice/rulers/rules, and the "other side" models trade well or become a second project.

### Which army is easiest to WIN with as a beginner?
Necrons. Their durability punishes beginner mistakes less — units come back after dying. Marines are close behind.

### Can I switch armies later?
Yes. Starter boxes resell easily, and lessons (rules, painting basics) transfer fully between factions.
