## The Holy Trinity

### 1. Quality Brushes (Kolinsky Sable)

You don't need 50 brushes. Three Kolinsky sable-hair brushes — #0, #1 and #2 — hold their point, carry paint properly and make detail work dramatically easier. A $15 Kolinsky outperforms a $40 synthetic set every time. Care matters more than brand: never let paint enter the ferrule, wash in cold water, use brush soap.

### 2. Wet Palette

The single most important tool. It keeps acrylic paint workable for hours (even days), holds your thinned mixes at perfect consistency, and saves enormous amounts of paint. Every frustration beginners blame on "skill" — chalky finishes, uneven coats — is usually paint drying on a dry palette.

### 3. Daylight Lamp

High-CRI daylight lighting removes shadows and shows true colours. Without it, you discover your "clean" highlights under room light — after they're dry. A clamp-on daylight lamp costs less than one miniature and changes every painting session.

## Prep Tools: Cutters, Files and Cleaning

- **Side cutters:** flush-cutting nippers remove parts from sprues without stress marks — the difference between a clean join and a filled gap
- **Hobby knife (#11 blade):** removes mould lines; a mould line running through a painted face ruins the model
- **Needle files:** smooth cut points where cutters can't reach
- **Mould-line scraper (optional):** faster and safer than a knife on flat surfaces

## Ergonomics and Desk Setup

Painting fatigue is real: hand tremor after 90 minutes ruins detail work.

- Sit with **elbows supported** on the desk
- **Model on a stand** at eye height, not flat on the table
- Paint in **45–60 minute sessions**, then break
- Keep a **magnifier or headband visor** for sub-2 mm details — cheap and transformative over 40

## FAQ

**Why do my brush tips curl?**
Paint has entered the ferrule (the metal band) and glued the hairs. Prevent it: never dip past two-thirds of the bristle length, wash in cold water, clean with brush soap after each session. A curled tip can sometimes be rescued with brush soap and warm — never hot — water.

**Are expensive brushes worth it for beginners?**
One good Kolinsky brush beats a cheap set — beginners press too hard, and good brushes are more forgiving. But no brush survives scrubbing; technique comes with time.

**Do I need a wet palette as a total beginner?**
Yes — it's the cheapest tool that fixes the most common beginner problem (drying paint). Improvise one with a food container, baking paper and a damp sponge if needed.

**What's the one tool most painters buy too late?**
A daylight lamp. Everyone buys it after a year of painting in yellow room light and discovering their colours were wrong.
