# How to Paint Glowing Plasma Effects on Miniatures

**Short answer:** Block in the glow with a dark base, add a midtone halo around the light source, then build a gradient of three brighter tones toward the centre and finish with pinpoint white highlights. Add reflected glow on nearby surfaces — that final step is what makes plasma effects look real instead of painted-on.

## What You Need

- Primer (black or very dark grey — glow needs a dark canvas)
- Base colour for the glow core (e.g. deep blue or violet)
- 2–3 lighter shades of the same hue (up to near-white for the hottest point)
- A dark contrast colour for the surrounding areas
- Fine detail brush (#0 or #1)
- Optional: glaze medium for smooth transitions

## Step 1: Choose the Light Source

Decide *before* painting where the light comes from: a plasma coil, a weapon barrel, glowing eyes, or a portal. One clear source beats three confused ones. Mark the spot with a dot of the brightest colour — this is your anchor for the entire gradient.

## Step 2: Block In the Glow

Paint the whole glow area in your darkest tone of the effect colour. Around it, sketch the midtone halo — the zone that will catch the light. Keep edges rough at this stage; you'll clean them up with the gradient.

## Step 3: Build the Gradient

Work from the outside in: darkest at the edges, brighter toward the source. Thin paint, multiple passes. The transition zone between two shades should be soft — drybrush or glaze the boundary if you see a hard step.

## Step 4: Add Reflected Light

This is the step most beginners skip — and the one that sells the effect. Take your midtone and add a few thin streaks on surfaces *near* the source: adjacent panels, the weapon housing, the ground below. If a surface would catch that light in reality, it needs a hint of it.

## Step 5: Final Highlights

Tiny, sharp dots of near-white at the very core. Small is critical — a big white blob kills the illusion in one brushstroke.

## Common Mistakes

- **Too bright too early** — you can always add brightness, but you can't remove it
- **Skipping reflected light** — the number-one reason glow looks flat
- **Warm and cool mixed** — pick one hue family per effect (green glow = green reflections)
- **Giant cores** — plasma coils are thin; keep the hottest point small

## FAQ

**Is OSL (object source lighting) the same as painting glow?**
OSL is the broader technique — painted glow is its simplest form. True OSL adds reflected light on all surrounding surfaces; this guide includes that in Step 4.

**Which colours work best for beginner plasma effects?**
Green and teal are the most forgiving — they're bright, contrast strongly with dark bases, and a slightly uneven blend still looks intentional.

**Can I paint glow effects over a light primer?**
It works but fights you: glow relies on dark surroundings for contrast. Prime dark, or paint a dark zone around the glow first.

**How do I make multiple glowing units look consistent?**
Mix one batch of each shade on a wet palette and paint all units in one session. Consistency between squads is what makes an army glow look deliberate.
