What You DON'T Need Yet

Beginners overspend on things that don't matter in month one:

  • Codex army books — the starter rules cover your models; codices matter at 1,000+ points
  • A full paint range — 3–4 pots plus a wash is plenty
  • Terrain — books, boxes and mugs make fine obstacles for your first games
  • A second army — the starter box already includes two sides

FAQ — Warhammer 40K Starter Sets

What is the best Warhammer 40K starter set for beginners?

The Ultimate Starter Set. It's the only box that includes two genuinely playable mini-armies plus the full rules, dice and mission deck — a complete hobby start in one purchase.

Is the smaller starter set worth it?

Yes, if you're unsure about the hobby or buying as a gift. It teaches assembly, painting and basic play for the lowest price. Expect to upgrade within weeks if you get hooked.

Do starter boxes include paints?

Typically no — paints are sold separately or in hobby bundles. Budget $30–40 for side cutters, glue (if needed), primer and 3–4 paint pots.

Can I play a real game with just a starter box?

Yes. The bigger boxes give you legal forces for 500-point games out of the box, which is exactly the size beginners should be playing anyway.

Should I buy a Combat Patrol box instead of a starter set?

If you already know which faction you want and someone can teach you the rules, a Combat Patrol box plus the free core rules is a great direct route. For most true beginners, the starter box is the better teacher.

Do starter box models count toward a real army?

Absolutely — every model in a starter box is a full legal unit you'll keep using for years. Nothing in a starter box is "disposable."
