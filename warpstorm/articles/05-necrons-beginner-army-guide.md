## Why Necrons Are a Perfect First Army

Necrons are ancient, undying robot legions — and mechanically, they're built for people learning the game:

1. **Reanimation Protocols forgive mistakes.** Your fallen warriors stand back up. While you're learning positioning, your army heals your errors.
2. **One simple army rule, clean datacards.** Fewer nested special rules than almost any other faction — you spend game nights learning 40K, not Necron exceptions.
3. **Fastest paint scheme in the game.** Silver basecoat + black wash + green accents = table-ready. No faction gets from sprue to tabletop faster.
4. **Small model count.** Most Necron lists run fewer models than horde armies — less assembly, less painting, less cost per finished game.

## Your First 1,000 Points (Simple Starter List)

| Unit | Role | Why It's Here |
|---|---|---|
| Overlord (or Royal Warden) | Leader, buffs Warriors | Cheap HQ that makes your core hit harder |
| 20x Necron Warriors | Battleline, objective holders | Your durable scoring core — reanimation keeps them alive |
| 3x Skorpekh Destroyers | Melee threat | Teaches aggressive play with a forgiving unit |
| Canoptek Scarab Swarms or Wraiths | Fast screen / speed | Movement-phase learning curve, cheap in points |
| 1x Heavy support (Annihilation Barge or similar) | Ranged firepower | Your first "big gun" — teaches shooting priority |

*Point values change with each dataslate — always check the current free points file. The structure (Warrior core + leader + one melee + one fast + one heavy) is durable across updates.*

**Expansion path:** add a Monolith or a C'tan Shard as your 2,000-point centrepiece. One big model per purchase — paint it before the next.

## Paint Scheme: The Weekend Army

The famous "one weekend" Necron recipe:

1. **Spray** black or grey primer
2. **Basecoat** everything silver (one coat is fine on metal)
3. **Wash** with black shade — the wash does the entire "depth" job
4. **Glow accents:** drybrush the weapon coils and eyes in green, add a second lighter pass on the brightest spots

Fifteen models in two evenings, and they look like a proper undying legion. Black-and-green variants, brass trims and rusty "tomb world" versions all come later — the base scheme is genuinely this easy.

## Playing Your First Games

- **Warriors in the middle.** They're your objectives team — march them up the board and let reanimation punish your opponent's shooting.
- **Shoot the dangerous stuff, ignore the chaff.** Necron guns are good; the beginner mistake is wasting them on cheap enemy units.
- **Don't fear melee.** Even when Destroyers die, that's their job — buying time for your Warriors to score.

## Common Necron Beginner Mistakes

- **Spending huge points on one giant unit too early** — learn with a balanced core first
- **Forgetting reanimation rolls** — it's the faction's whole identity; write it on your army roster
- **Leaving the Overlord's buff out of range** — keep leaders glued to the Warriors

## FAQ — Necrons for Beginners

**Are Necrons good for beginners?**
Yes — arguably the best non-Marine start. Simple rules, durable units and a fast paint scheme make them ideal while you're learning movement, shooting and objectives.

**How much does a 1,000-point Necron army cost?**
Around $250–350 including paints and tools. The Necrons half of a starter box plus one or two boxes gets you most of the way there.

**What's the easiest Necron paint scheme?**
Silver basecoat, black wash, glowing green accents. It's the most famous beginner scheme in the hobby for a reason — fifteen models in a weekend, looking genuinely good.

**Necrons vs. Space Marines for a first army?**
Both excellent. Marines: maximum community support and starter availability. Necrons: better looks-per-hour-of-painting and fewer models to manage. Read our [full comparison](/best-warhammer-40k-army-for-beginners/).

**Do Necrons have complicated rules?**
Their core rules are simple — reanimation is one mechanic, and most units are straightforward shooting or melee. You'll spend more time learning 40K's universal rules than Necron-specific exceptions.

**What do I buy after the starter box?**
One Warrior box and one fast or melee box gets you to 1,000 points. Add your first big centrepiece (Monolith or C'tan) as the reward for finishing your paint queue.
